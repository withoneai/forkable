# Calendar apps landscape, for adding a calendar to Orez

Research date 2026-10-22. Sources are linked inline. **[unverified]** means one secondary source only, a vendor/competitor blog, or my own knowledge with no fresh confirmation. Prices change often. Re-check before quoting any of them in public copy.

Caution on sources: a lot of the 2026 "review" content comes from competitors' blogs (Morgen, Carly/usecarly, ClickUp, Cal.com, Saner). I used them for facts that other sources back up, and flagged the rest.

## 1. Market state, October 2026 (what changed recently)

- **Clockwise is dead.** The team was acquihired by Salesforce for Agentforce. It was announced around March 19-23, 2026, and the service **shut down March 27, 2026**. All customer data was deleted, prepaid plans were refunded pro rata, and users were pointed to Reclaim, which matched Clockwise's prices. ([TechRadar via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/salesforce-recuits-team-behind-calendar-103000806.html), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/salesforce-acquires-clockwise-for-talent-kills-76m-calendar-ai-product-in-one-week), [Carly, competitor](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/clockwise-shut-down/))
- **Notion Mail shut down September 22, 2026.** Its `/schedule` availability-in-composer flow went with it. Notion Calendar itself is still live and free. ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/notion-mail-is-shutting-down-3681674/), [Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-mail-inbox-is-going-away-what-to-do-next))
- **Rise Calendar shut down March 31, 2025.** It cited a lack of traction and competition from Clockwise, Reclaim and Cron. ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/1/rise-calendar-is-shutting-down-users-are-urged-to-export-their-data-by-march-31-2025))
- **Cal.com went closed source in April 2026.** The open code is now `calcom/cal.diy` under MIT, without teams, round-robin, routing forms, workflows or SSO. The Cal.diy site recommends it for personal, non-production use. ([Cal.com blog](https://cal.com/blog/cal-diy-open-source-to-closed-source), [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/cal-com-goes-proprietary/), [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/4/cal-com-is-going-closed-source-with-a-major-shift-in-its-license-strategy/))
- **Google is moving into scheduling-from-email.** Gmail "Help me schedule" launched October 2025 and gained group support in March 2026. Calendar "Suggested times" arrived January 2026. Gemini Spark, a 24/7 agent with Gmail and Calendar access, was announced at I/O on May 19, 2026. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/14/googles-gemini-can-now-help-you-schedule-google-calendar-meetings/), [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/03/google-gmail-help-me-schedule-group-meetings-update.html), [Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/01/improved-meeting-suggestions-gemini-calendar.html), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/368389/google-gemini-spark-ai-agent-challenge-hermes-openclaw?amp=1))
- **Calendly added AI.** In March 2026 it shipped an MCP server. In August 2026 came **Callie** (beta), an assistant you CC into any email thread, plus Calendly Notetaker. ([destinationCRM](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Across-the-Wire/Calendly-Launches-Callie-an-AI-Assistant-and-Notetaker-176229.aspx), [Calendly newsroom](https://calendly.com/newsroom/press-release/calendly-new-ai-product-suite))
- **New entrant: Blockit** (January 2026, $5M seed led by Sequoia). Its agents negotiate meeting times with other agents. You bring it in by CC on email or a Slack message. It costs $1,000/yr per individual and $5,000/yr per team. A co-founder previously worked on Timeful, Google Calendar and Clockwise. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/former-sequoia-partners-new-startup-uses-ai-to-negotiate-your-calendar-for-you/))
- **The pattern:** standalone "AI auto-scheduler" companies are struggling (Clockwise and Rise are dead, Motion pivoted to "AI employees"). The energy is moving to **scheduling inside email** (Superhuman, Gmail Help me schedule, Calendly Callie, Blockit, Shortwave). That is the Orez opening.

## 2. App by app

### Notion Calendar (ex-Cron)
- **Best at:** speed and keyboard flow on top of Google Calendar (Outlook added later). Free. ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/category/notion-calendar))
- **Keyboard:** Cmd/Ctrl+K command menu. 1-9 sets the number of visible days. D/W/M switch views. T jumps to today. P overlays a teammate's calendar. S shares availability. F starts an instant call. O imports a Notion database. Space in a new event takes a natural-language description. ([Matthias Frank guide](https://matthiasfrank.de/en/notion-calendar/))
- **Natural language:** typed into a new event, it fills in title, time and participants. ([same](https://matthiasfrank.de/en/notion-calendar/))
- **Availability:** press S, drag across free gaps, and get a link. Notion's 2024 notes call it a built-in scheduling tool. One guide says the links are **single use**, and that the plain-text snippets don't re-check later events, so you can get double booked. ([Notion release 2.37](https://www.notion.com/releases/2024-01-17), [Frank](https://matthiasfrank.de/en/notion-calendar/))
- **Time zones:** multiple zones with custom labels, plus "Time Travel" to temporarily view another zone. The primary zone is taken from Google at setup, and later changes don't sync either way. ([Notion help, Time zones](https://www.notion.com/help/time-zones))
- **Inside email:** through Notion Mail's `/schedule`: drag availability in a mini calendar and a link is inserted ([help](https://notion.com/help/schedule-meetings-with-notion-mail)). This is gone since Mail's shutdown on September 22, 2026.
- **AI:** AI Meeting Notes tied to calendar events. Speaker labels arrived July 2026. Custom Agents can run after a note finishes (July 31, 2026). ([Notion releases 2026-07-01](https://www.notion.com/releases/2026-07-01), [2026-07-31](https://www.notion.com/releases/2026-07-31)). "Smart scheduling" suggested times when adding attendees, reported September 2026 **[unverified]**.
- **Complaints (HN, January 2024):** scary Google permission scopes, Google-only sign-in at launch, no offline mode, a slow mobile app, bugs since the acquisition, a rough Cron migration, distrust of Notion lock-in. Praise: fast, clean, global join-meeting shortcut, easy availability sharing. ([HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39030375)). Later reviews cite no Android app and ecosystem lock-in **[unverified, single editorial source]** ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/fantastical-google-apple-outlook-notion-calendar-apps-ranked-2026)).

### Superhuman (Mail + Calendar, now under the Superhuman suite, ex-Grammarly)

([Morgen review, competitor](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/superhuman-pricing), [efficient.app](https://efficient.app/compare/superhuman-vs-amie))

Cmd+K then **?** (Ask AI) creates an event from a natural-language prompt (Business/Enterprise). **Cmd+Shift+A** shares availability: pick slots, and live slots plus a booking link drop into the reply. **M** opens Find Time with a teammate (September 2025). ([Superhuman help, Create Event](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/38458504388627-Create-Event), [AlternativeTo on Find Time](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/9/superhuman-launches-find-time-for-inline-team-availability-and-faster-scheduling))
- **Availability in reply:** the slots are live and update if your schedule changes. Recipients book without a Superhuman account. You can add teammates' availability and show only mutual free slots, or all slots for an important meeting. Superhuman's reason for building it: scheduling takes **three emails on average**. ([TechCrunch, December 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/04/superhuman-launches-availablity-sharing-as-it-thinks-about-building-a-calendar-app))
- **AI:** Superhuman Go suggests actions from email, CRM or project context ("schedule a meeting", "add a reminder") and recalls previous meeting notes plus prep topics. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/29/grammarly-transforms-ai-enabled-productivity-suite-superhuman-rebrand/), [Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/collaboration-productivity/grammarly-rebrands-as-superhuman-introduces-unified-ai-suite/)). The mobile apps were redesigned September 1, 2026 with Mail / Calendar / Search tabs **[single source]**.
- **Pricing:** Mail Starter $30/mo ($25 annual) and Business $40 ($33). There is no Mail free tier. Sources conflict on which tier includes calendar features **[unverified]**. ([Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/superhuman-pricing))

## 3. Synthesis

### (a) Table stakes: a credible calendar must have these
1. Day, week (with a 1-9 day count, or at least day/3-day/week), month and agenda views. Fast. Keyboard: c create, t today, j/k or arrows, d/w/m, a command palette, Esc, ? for help. Match Google's keys where possible so muscle memory carries over.
2. Read and write Google Calendar, multiple calendars with show/hide, colors, all-day events, recurring events (edit this / this and following / all), reminders.
3. Natural-language quick create ("lunch w/ Sam fri 1pm 45m at Tartine"), with a preview before saving.
4. RSVP (Yes / No / Maybe) with an optional note, and "propose new time".
5. Meet link on create (conferenceData). Detect and show join links for Zoom, Teams and others, and a one-key "join next meeting".
6. Time zones: a secondary zone column, per-event start and end zones, travel-aware display, and correct DST. This is the top cross-app complaint.
7. Share availability: inline times in a reply plus an optional booking link that re-checks free/busy at booking time (avoid Notion's double-booking trap).
8. Free/busy for attendees when picking a time (within the org, or calendars shared with you).
9. Notifications for upcoming events. Offline-tolerant reads. Live updates (push plus sync).
10. Spam-invite handling (unknown senders do not auto-land on the calendar).

### (b) Where email plus calendar together beat either alone (the Orez angle)
1. **Schedule from the thread.** One key (Superhuman's B) drafts an event from the open thread: attendees from To/Cc, title and agenda summarized from the thread, duration and time proposed from both sides' availability and any times mentioned in the thread. The event description links back to the thread.
2. **Availability in the reply.** While composing, typing "next week" or "Tuesday" opens a side calendar. Pick slots and insert them as clean text (Vimcal-style drag-and-copy, which works for any recipient with no link to click) plus an optional live booking link. Times are shown in the recipient's time zone, inferred from earlier messages, signature or Date headers. Superhuman's "3 emails to schedule a meeting" stat is the pitch.
3. **Smart invite cards in the inbox.** For a `text/calendar` METHOD:REQUEST email, show a card with RSVP, conflicts at that time ("overlaps with 1:1 with Dana"), and a mini day view. For a CANCEL, show a struck-through card and archive the email. For updates, show a diff ("moved from 2pm to 3pm"). After you RSVP, the invite email can auto-archive. This brings the email noise of calendar traffic to near zero.
4. **"They replied with a time" detection.** When a reply says "Thursday 3pm works", offer a one-key "Book it" that creates the event with the people on the thread and sends a confirmation reply. This closes the loop Help me schedule only half-closes.
5. **Meeting prep briefs drawn from mail.** Before each meeting, show "who these people are, the last 3 threads with them, open asks, attachments mentioned". Gmail and Calendar don't do this natively (it needs a Workspace Studio flow), and email history is exactly what Orez already indexes. Pair it with VIP and goals context.
6. **Post-meeting follow-up.** After an event ends, draft the follow-up email to attendees, prefilled with the agenda from the invite thread. Notion added a version of this through meeting notes. Orez can do it without a notetaker.
7. **Inbox triage that knows your day.** Lanes and the morning brief include "meetings today and the threads you need to read before them" and "invites waiting for your answer". "Needs reply" can rank higher for people you meet today.
8. **Unsubscribe-level control over calendar noise.** Auto-decline or auto-hide invites from unknown or marketing senders (Jev judgment) before they hit the calendar. This targets the known Google spam-invite problem.

### (c) What AI genuinely adds vs gimmicks
**Genuine (users keep these):**
- Extracting structured events from messy text: the email body, a forwarded flyer, "can we do Tues or Wed after 2?". Fantastical, Dola and Superhuman all ship this, and it is a classic structured judgment (fits Jev).
- Choosing candidate slots with soft constraints (time zones, working hours, buffers, VIP priority) and writing them in a natural reply.
- Summaries for prep briefs and follow-ups grounded in your own threads, with citations (Orez already cites in Ask your inbox).
- Classifying invites (real vs spam vs FYI) and deciding which need action.

**Gimmicks or risky:**
- Fully autonomous rescheduling of your week. Motion's "reshuffling" is a top complaint. Clockwise's auto-moves were loved, but needed team-wide adoption and still died. Keep humans approving changes. Morgen's "propose, then Schedule all" is the right pattern.
- An AI that sends invites or RSVPs without confirmation. Google's own Spark disclaimer shows the risk, and it fits Orez's grant story badly (writes must be explicit and scoped).
- Chat-only calendars ("talk to your calendar") as the main interface. It is fine as an add-on palette command and slower than direct manipulation for most edits.
- Generic "focus time" blocks with no task context (Google already gives Focus Time free).
- Agent-to-agent negotiation (Blockit) is interesting but needs both sides adopting it. Watch it, don't build it.

### (d) Google Calendar API realities that shape the build
- **Incremental sync.** Do a full `events.list` (optionally bounded by `timeMin`, e.g. one year back), page through, and store `nextSyncToken` (present only on the last page). Then call with `syncToken`. On **410 Gone**, wipe that calendar's local store and do a full re-sync. Every page of an incremental pass must repeat the exact same params. `syncToken` **cannot** be combined with `iCalUID`, `orderBy`, `privateExtendedProperty`, `q`, `sharedExtendedProperty`, `timeMin`, `timeMax`, `updatedMin`. Incremental results always include deleted entries (status `cancelled`). `maxResults` defaults to 250, max 2500. ([Sync guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/sync), [events.list](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events/list))
  - Consequence: you cannot sync with `singleEvents=true` plus a time window **and** use `syncToken` the way you would a windowed query. The usual design syncs the raw series plus exceptions with the token and expands recurrences locally (RRULE library), or keeps a separate bounded `singleEvents=true` query for the visible range. Note that `showDeleted` and `singleEvents` interact oddly for cancelled instances (see the list docs).
- **Push (watch).** `events.watch` per calendar, plus `calendarList.watch` once per user. HTTPS with a valid certificate is required. The webhook has **no body**, only headers (`X-Goog-Channel-ID`, `X-Goog-Resource-State` = sync/exists/not_exists, `X-Goog-Channel-Token`, `X-Goog-Message-Number`). On each ping, run an incremental sync. Channels expire (ttl default 604800s = 7 days) with **no auto-renew**, so re-watch with a new id and expect overlap. Stop channels with `channels/stop`(id, resourceId). Google says "Notifications are not 100% reliable", so keep a periodic sync anyway. ([Push guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/push), [events.watch](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events/watch?hl=hi)). For Orez hosted, this fits the planned Cron, Queue and Durable Object design: the webhook enqueues a sync for that calendar, and the 2-minute cron is the fallback. For local `npx` mode there is no public HTTPS endpoint, so poll with the syncToken (cheap) or relay through the hosted worker.
- **Free/busy.** `POST /freeBusy` with timeMin, timeMax, `items[].id`, and optional `timeZone` (UTC default). Max 50 calendars (`calendarExpansionMax`) and 100 group members (`groupExpansionMax`). Errors per calendar: `notFound`, `tooManyCalendarsRequested`, `groupTooBig`. Busy ranges are start-inclusive, end-exclusive. It returns data only for calendars the user can see. **External people at other companies generally come back with errors or nothing**, so for outsiders you can only propose your own free time ([freebusy.query](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/freebusy/query); the external-visibility behavior is from experience and Gemini's documented same-org limit **[partly unverified]**).
- **Meet links.** On insert or patch, pass `conferenceDataVersion=1` and `conferenceData.createRequest{requestId: <unique>, conferenceSolutionKey:{type:"hangoutsMeet"}}`. The response may show `status: pending`, so re-read until `success`. Check `allowedConferenceSolutionTypes` on the calendar. Don't reuse Meet data across events. Google warns that apps storing events locally must do a full sync before enabling conference support, or existing conferences may be dropped. ([Create events](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/create-events))
- **Sending invites.** Writes take `sendUpdates=all|externalOnly|none`. Use `all` for real invites. You can set your own event `id` (5-1024 chars, base32hex a-v0-9) for idempotent retries. ([Events resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events))
- **Recurring events.** `recurrence[]` holds RRULE/EXDATE/RDATE lines. `timeZone` is **required** on start and end for recurring events. Instances carry `recurringEventId` plus `originalStartTime` (stable identity even if moved). `events.instances` lists one series. Editing one instance creates an exception. "This and following" means splitting the series yourself: trim the original RRULE with UNTIL and create a new series. Google warns against editing instance by instance. ([Recurring guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/recurringevents))
- **Attendees and RSVP.** `attendees[].responseStatus` is one of needsAction / accepted / declined / tentative. `self` marks the current user and `organizer` the organizer. To RSVP, PATCH the event on **your own** calendar, changing only your attendee entry. Only the response status propagates back to the organizer. Respect `attendeesOmitted`. `guestsCanModify` defaults to false. Event types: default, focusTime, outOfOffice, workingLocation, birthday, fromGmail (fromGmail can't be created by the API). Filter with `eventTypes`. ([Events](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events), [Inviting attendees](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/concepts/inviting-attendees-to-events))
- **ICS invites in Gmail.** Invites travel as iMIP (RFC 6047). A `text/calendar; method=REQUEST` part, often inside multipart/alternative, usually with an `invite.ics` attachment. Replies use METHOD:REPLY and cancellations METHOD:CANCEL. ([RFC 6047](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6047), [Nodemailer docs](https://nodemailer.com/message/calendar-events))
  - **How Orez should handle them [design inference]:** parse the ICS UID and SEQUENCE. Look up the event with `events.list?iCalUID=<UID>` on primary (Google-created events have `iCalUID` = `<id>@google.com`). If found (Google auto-added it), RSVP by patching `self.responseStatus`, and Google sends the reply email. If it is not found (unknown sender under the "Only if sender is known" setting, or the user set "When I respond in email"), offer "Add to calendar" with `events.import` (needs the iCalUID and the organizer). An RSVP from Orez must then send an iTIP REPLY email to the organizer through Gmail, because import does not notify anyone. Use SEQUENCE to ignore stale updates. Show CANCEL as cancelled. ([Inviting attendees](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/concepts/inviting-attendees-to-events), [Google help, manage invitations](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/13159188))
- **Quotas.** 10,000 requests per minute per project and **600 per minute per user per project** (sliding window). A 1,000,000 per day per project billing threshold. Over-quota returns 403 or 429 `usageLimits`. Use exponential backoff with jitter (max 32-64s), and randomize sync timing ±25%. ([Quota](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/quota)). Account-level limits: about 10,000 external invites in a short period, about 2,000 emails to external guests per 24h, 100,000 events created, 60 calendars created, 750 shares. ([Workspace help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/calendar/avoid-calendar-use-limits))
- **Scopes.** Calendar scopes (`calendar`, `calendar.events`, `calendar.readonly`, `calendar.freebusy`) are Google "sensitive" scopes, unlike Gmail read/modify, which are "restricted" and need a CASA assessment **[from knowledge, re-verify on Google's scope list]**. Request the narrowest set. HN users balked at Notion Calendar's scary scope screen ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39030375)). Under One Connect, the grant's access level (Read only vs Read & write) maps cleanly onto "show calendar" vs "create events and RSVP", so a declined write shows up as a visible `Google Calendar · write declined, 403` receipt.
- **Available through One (checked with `one actions find google-calendar`, 2026-10-22):** List events, Get, Create, Patch, Update, Quick Add, Import, Move, List Instances, Free/Busy, Watch events, Watch calendarList, Watch settings, Watch ACL, Stop channel, calendarList, ACL, and create secondary calendar. So sync, watch and freebusy can all run on the user's Connect grant, consistent with Orez's no-key rule. The watch webhook address must be Orez's own hosted HTTPS endpoint.
- **Outlook later [from knowledge, unverified here]:** Microsoft Graph uses delta queries (`/me/calendarView/delta`) and change subscriptions that expire in about 3 days (event subscriptions max about 4,230 minutes) and need a validation handshake. Design the sync layer around "change ping, then token-based delta" so both providers fit.
